# Crit 9 reflection

*Note to the marker: I missed crit 9 itself (I was unwell), so there's no
crit-9 session to reflect on directly. This file is a complement written
after the fact — its content covers work done after crit 10, not crit 9's
own session.*

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Requiring checkable primary sources, building on crit 10, made arguments
concrete. The deeper move was bringing in a model not trained by
Anthropic and comparing its answers against this one's on the same
questions — a direct test of the research note's own claim that two
checks only count as independent if they actually fail differently, not
just because they come from different models. They didn't agree on
everything; where they diverged is where I went and checked myself, and
that's where the external audit report and several later ideas came from.
The disagreement itself is a small, live confirmation of the finding: it
doesn't mean agreement would have meant nothing to find, only that
agreement alone wouldn't have given me the same reason to go check.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This work is a small answer to the course's bigger question: what is a
human still for, once AI can search faster and hold more detail than I
ever will. AI took the painful part off me — it can read a paper in
seconds and never forgets a citation's page number. It can even ask me
things I'd never have thought to ask myself. But a surfaced question is
only useful if I'm equipped to recognize it's worth answering, and to know
roughly where to go looking — and that recognition has to come from
somewhere I've already been, not from the question itself. Without my own
reading in social and crowd psychology already sitting in my head, I
wouldn't even have known that question was worth chasing, let alone where
to point the search. Without it, the only way through is to spend far
more tokens wandering — asking AI to search broadly, report back, and
let me feel out a direction by trial — in place of the years of
accumulated reading a person could instead already carry in. AI can
close the information gap; it can't close the gap in lived accumulation
that tells me which question actually matters. That's what I want to keep
building for myself, not delegate.
