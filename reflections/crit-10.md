# Crit 10 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was realizing that a citation can be entirely accurate and
still be wrong for the project citing it. Twice, independently, an agent
reached for Robin Sloan's "home-cooked app" essay and Aral Balkan's "What is
the Small Web?" to justify a design — once in my own repo, once in
shitao's, with no shared context between the sessions. Neither essay's
actual argument had anything to do with either app's mechanic; what
connected them was surface resemblance, the kind of project these two
essays get cited for. A similar, smaller version happened again later, in a
citation that overclaimed what a theory actually argued. This is the real
risk: an agent can combine ideas that sound like they belong together
without ever establishing that they logically do, and the result reads
just as confident either way. Fluent, well-cited language doesn't mean the
connection was checked — that check is still a human job.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I'd earlier asked a lecturer how the teaching team can tell whether a
student is actually thinking critically when they agree with an agent's
output, versus just accepting it. My own reasoning at the time was that
rejection is the easier signal: if a student pushes back, they clearly read
and thought enough to notice something was off. Acceptance proves nothing
either way — it looks identical whether the student checked carefully or
didn't look at all.

This week complicated that answer rather than confirming it. I accepted the
agent's citation fixes outright — I didn't reject them. But accepting them
still left me noticing a resemblance to the earlier Sloan/Balkan pattern,
which is what made me ask "why" at all. That "why" didn't change the output
I'd already accepted; what it changed was surfacing a more general problem
in how the agent works, one instance wouldn't have shown on its own. So the
real signal isn't acceptance versus rejection — it's whether a human kept
noticing and questioning *after* accepting, not just at the moment of
deciding yes or no. That's a harder thing to see from outside a
conversation than a simple accept/reject would be, but it's the part that
actually matters.
